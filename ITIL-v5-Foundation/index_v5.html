Module 1

Key Terms & Definitions

Foundational concepts defining the language of modern digital service management.

Value Co-Creation

Value is no longer delivered in a unidirectional flow. It is co-created through an active collaboration between providers and consumers, as well as other organizations that are part of the relevant service relationships.

Service

A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.

Service Relationship

The cooperation between a service provider and service consumer. It includes service provision, service consumption, and service relationship management.

Product

A configuration of an organization’s resources designed to offer value for a consumer. Products are complex systems that include hardware, software, and people.

Service Offering

A formal description of one or more services, designed to address the needs of a target consumer group. It may include goods, access to resources, and service actions.

Organization

A person or a group of people that has its own functions with responsibilities, authorities, and relationships to achieve its objectives.

Module 2

The Four Dimensions

To ensure a holistic approach to service management, ITIL v5 emphasizes four dimensions. Failing to address any one of these can result in services that are undeliverable or fail to meet expectations.

  • 1 Organizations & People: Roles, skills, culture, and organizational structure.
  • 2 Information & Technology: The technology stacks, databases, AI systems, and information architectures used.
  • 3 Partners & Suppliers: Relationships, contracts, and dependencies on external vendors.
  • 4 Value Streams & Processes: The workflows and activities that convert inputs into outputs.

External Factors (PESTLE)

All four dimensions are constrained or influenced by external factors: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental.

Module 4

The ITIL Service Value System (SVS)

The SVS represents how all the components and activities of the organization work together to facilitate value creation.

Governance

The means by which the organization is directed and controlled.

Service Value Chain

The central element: An operating model which outlines the key activities required to respond to demand.

Practices

Sets of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective.

Continual Improvement

A recurring organizational activity performed at all levels to ensure that organization's performance aligns with expectations.

Module 5

Value Stream Management

Identifying and mapping the series of steps an organization undertakes to create and deliver products and services.

Identification
Recognizing specific streams (e.g., "New User Onboarding" or "Incident Resolution") rather than isolating departments.
Mapping
Visualizing the current state (as-is) to identify bottlenecks, waste, and wait times.
Optimization
Applying the principle "Optimize and Automate" to streamline the flow from demand to value.
